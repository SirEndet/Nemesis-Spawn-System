# Nemesis Spawn System

A recurring Monolith hunter for S.T.A.L.K.E.R. Anomaly / GAMMA.

Every so often, a hunter comes for you. He spawns out of sight, sprints after you and is very hard to kill. Hurt him badly enough and he teleports away in a Monolith burst. He always comes back, and each time he is stronger.

## What happens in a hunt

- **The warning.** Radio static tells you something is coming (you can also turn on a strange PDA message). A short while later the hunt begins.
- **The hunter.** He spawns 80 to 140 m away, at a spot where nobody else is waiting if there is one (else where the fewest are), and heads straight for you. Named hunters carry a Monolith rank in their name (Acolyte, Disciple, Zealot, Sentinel, Templar, Herald), so you can tell them apart from ordinary stalkers.
- **His squad.** Sometimes he brings company: up to four more Monolith stalkers. Some of them can be named hunters too. The rest are normal stalkers you can kill, and they keep hunting you even after the named hunters are gone.
- **The fight.** When he sees you, he fights like any GAMMA stalker, only a better shot. Named hunters can't be killed: when their health gets low, they run for cover and teleport out.
- **What you kill is yours.** Regular squad members and the boss leave a body to loot when you kill them, even when someone else lands the last shot right after yours. Those killed by others vanish. An option (on by default) keeps NPCs off your kills.
- **He learns.** Named hunters wear armour like yours, and every hunt you beat makes the next one a little tougher. Outlast a hunt and he comes back one step weaker. If he kills you, he loses half his strength.
- **Psy hum.** A named hunter carries a psy hum with him: you can hear where he is, and it grows as he comes closer.
- **You hear them land.** When a hunt arrives, the Monolith's call sounds from where they appear (Monolith hunts), and a psy swell crosses your screen, heavier each cycle. Before the radio warning, the world falls silent for a moment.
- **He remembers.** Beat a hunter somewhere and they remember the spot for a week. Fight from there again and they know exactly where you are.
- **He comes back.** A named hunter you make break off can lead a later hunt, by name and one rank higher, and he tells you where you last met (see *The Returning* below).
- **Buildings.** Hide inside and they don't come in after you straight away. They wait outside for a minute and a half, then storm in. Shoot at them from inside and they storm in at once.
- **Escaping.** Survive long enough (6 in-game hours by default) and the hunters give up.
- **No running to another map.** Change maps any way you like and they follow: a minute or two after you arrive, the same hunters teleport in, still as hurt as you left them, from the side you came in on. They follow twice by default; change maps once more and you've shaken them off.

**No safe havens, but some rules.** A hunt never starts while you are in a base or trader camp that is friendly or neutral to you, in a dialogue, asleep, or on an underground map. Squads are smaller in rookie areas like Cordon and Garbage, and in the far north more of them are named hunters. Enemy bases are no shelter. Once the warning has come through, though, the hunt is on: walking into a base won't stop it.

**Playing as Monolith?** Then the Military hunt you, with army ranks (or UNISG, if you pick them in the menu). **Zombified or Sin?** Then the hunters are UNISG, with their own ranks.

### The boss

Rarely, a hunter is the boss: **Bogdan Kravets**, for UNISG hunts **Lyutomyr Kat**, for the Military **Taras Hrytsenko** (you can rename all three). He is tougher, always cloaked, has his own look and messages, and wants you above everyone else. By default, three named hunters keep you busy while he hunts you, and you'll know he's here when they teleport in. While cloaked he slips past everyone unnoticed; only your shot gives him away. Stand still and he sneaks around behind you; run and he runs you down. Within 80 m a heartbeat starts in your head, faster the closer he gets. He tells you before he strikes, and his first two shots at you always miss. He never retreats: he fights to the death, and his body is yours to loot. Kill him yourself and a rare stash is marked on your PDA. The longer he stays away, the likelier he gets.

### The emission boss

When an emission meets a hunt (and rarely, in any emission), the hunt is called off and **Ilarion Hrech**, a Sin (UNISG if you play Monolith, Zombified or Sin), comes instead. Get into a building that shelters you from the emission and stay inside a few seconds: a ring of ghosts rises around you, and two or three more stand across every door. Try to leave and they push you back, each push costing a little psy health. They hold you there while the emission rages. As its roar dies down, lightning strikes outside and he teleports in. Twenty seconds before him, a horde of zombies (snorks further north) comes out of the dark beyond the ghosts and closes in, some of them from the other side of your shelter, and they tear into anyone they meet on the way. He walks in through the emission that kills everyone else. Other stalkers barely scratch him. The ghosts keep you in until his hunt ends. Like the other boss, he fights to the death. Either boss will notice when you reload close to him.

### The Thunder Boss

**Danylo Raskat**, a Loner against the world, arrives alone in five lightning strikes, wearing an Exo-SEVA suit with electric sparks around his legs. Every faction fights him, Loners too, and he fights everyone near him, you included. Killing him, or anyone he kills, costs no standing with any faction. He has no cloak or sneak attack. After your first hunt, each thunderstorm has a 15% chance to bring him, with at least five in-game days between visits. He stays away during another hunt, an emission or psy storm, and for five real minutes after either storm ends.

Before casting, his sparks grow stronger and a buzz gives you 1.5 seconds of warning. He then stops, puts his weapon away and raises his hand. The first bolts follow 1.5 seconds later. Three volleys strike three seconds apart. Keep moving: bolts aimed at you land where you were half a second earlier, each taking 7% of your current health if it reaches you. Other volleys can strike nearby enemy stalkers or mutants; the final bolt of an NPC volley kills its target. Story NPCs, companions, traders and Nemesis hunters are protected from these strikes. If no suitable NPC is available, another volley can target you.

While his hand is up, he takes 90% less damage and regains 4% of his full health each second. Attack between casts. Your kill, or a kill shortly after your hit, leaves his body and gear; an NPC kill without your assist sends him away in lightning. His sounds use Lightning volume on General. His settings have their own page and are kept separate from difficulty presets.

### The Returning

The hunters you don't finish come back. Every named hunter starts at his faction's lowest title and the game's rank Experienced. One who breaks off because of your hits is remembered, with the map you met on; a later hunt can be led by him (35% by default), one title and one rank higher each time, and he opens with his own line ("You saw me fall at Garbage..."). With AlifeTactics installed, its own skill curves read his rank; without it, Nemesis's three sliders make him a better shot, quicker to notice you and longer in his bursts. At the top rank, **Legend**, he waits, then comes for you alone, as a boss. Kill a hunter and his name is gone for good. Kill them all...

### The Hunt's Eye

Three things that tell you they are coming, and make it worse to linger:
- **Signs before the hunt.** An in-game hour before a hunt, you intercept their chatter on your PDA, and you know who is coming. The hunt waits until that hour has passed: use it.
- **The Mark.** What you do to the hunters brands you: a boss you kill most, a named hunter you kill or make break off less, a regular hunt member you kill a little. While you are marked, the next hunt comes sooner. It fades with time; sleep eases it, an anti-psy drug (Psy-Block, or anything that protects from psy) removes 35% of the current Mark by default. Your PDA tells you when it takes hold and when it is gone.
- **Targeted Ground.** Every hunt on a map, every hunter who gets away from you there, and every half day you stay, they target that map more. A targeted map brings its hunts sooner, and they know your old spots from further away. It cools three times as fast once you leave, drops with every map change and with an anti-psy drug: keep moving. Your PDA tells you when the map you are on is targeted, and when it has cooled.

## Settings

Everything is in **MCM → Nemesis Spawn System**, on nine pages: General, Hunts, Combat, Boss, Emission boss, The Returning, The Hunt's Eye, Thunder Boss and Debug & tools.

### Difficulty

The **Difficulty** setting at the top of the General page picks Easy, Medium (the default) or Hard in one go. It sets the sliders in the difficulty table; check boxes, volumes, effects and Thunder Boss settings are left alone. Medium is the defaults, Easy is about 20% easier and Hard about 20% harder. A pick applies at once, with no Apply needed (Cancel does not undo it). If you change one of these settings by hand afterwards, Difficulty shows Custom. When you switch from Custom to Easy, Medium or Hard, your own values, even ones not applied yet, are saved to the Automatic Backup. Switching between Easy, Medium and Hard leaves that backup alone, so **Load settings from Automatic Backup** always brings back your last Custom settings. Signs lead is the same on every difficulty.

## Requirements

- **G.A.M.M.A.** (or Anomaly 1.5.3 with MCM).
- **The latest modded exes** by **themrdemonized** ([GitHub](https://github.com/themrdemonized/xray-monolith)). GAMMA's installer keeps them up to date. You need at least the build of 15 September 2026. On an older one, with GAMMA's ballistics, your shots can kill named hunters instead of making them break off, and the mod tells you so once on your PDA. The exes' scripts must also load: they are supplied in `db/mods/00_modded_exes_gamedata.db0`. Older loose copies or conflicting mod files can override them; a new enough exe alone does not guarantee that those scripts are active.
- **xlibs 1.9.0 or newer** by **Damian Sirbu** ([GitHub](https://github.com/damiansirbu-stalker/xlibs) · [ModDB](https://www.moddb.com/mods/stalker-anomaly/addons/xlibs-1001)). GAMMA already includes it as **482- Xlib - damian_sirbu**, switched off: tick it in Mod Organizer.

Works alongside AlifePlus, AlifeTactics, Wearable Devices, the Wearable Devices PDA mod and Obrien GAMMA Overhaul, but does not need them.

## Install and update

Install with Mod Organizer like any other mod and tick it. Keep the current Nemesis files and modded-exes scripts active when resolving file conflicts. Settings appear under MCM → Nemesis Spawn System.

The mod is also in Russian: if your game runs in Russian, its menu and every message are in Russian too, and `Nemesis_README_ru.md` is this guide in Russian.

To update, install the new version over the old one and choose *Replace*. Your saves and settings carry over. Don't update in the middle of a hunt, while hunters are following you, or while the emission boss is on his way.

## Uninstalling

**Use a save made while no hunt was running.** A save made during a hunt holds the hunt's squad, which only this mod can load, so without the mod it will most likely not load.

1. In MCM → Nemesis Spawn System → Debug & tools, tick **Prepare for uninstall** and apply. Any running hunt is called off and every Nemesis squad leaves the world. Within about 10 seconds a PDA message says it is safe to save.
2. Save.
3. Disable or remove the mod, and load that save.

(Without the box: finish the hunt by beating it or letting it time out, wait until the hunters are gone, then save.) If you keep the mod after all, untick the box and the hunts come back.

The mod keeps one small file of its own, `gamedata/configs/nss_player.ltx` (with Mod Organizer, in its overwrite folder): your death record and your settings backup. Before 1.0.3 it was called `nwss_player.ltx`: on the first load the mod carries its contents over to the new file, and from the next start it removes the old one. You can delete the file after uninstalling. Older versions may also have left `gamedata/nemesis_death_reset.txt`, which can go too.

## Reporting a bug

Turn on **Debug logs**, play until it happens, and send both logs from `appdata/logs/` in your game folder: the game's `xray_<name>.log` and Nemesis's own `nemesis.log`. If you ever see a yellow **NEMESIS IMPORTANT ERROR (please report)** line, it shows even with Debug logs off and may mean a broken save or install: please send that log.
